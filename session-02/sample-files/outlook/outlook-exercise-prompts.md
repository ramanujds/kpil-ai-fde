# Copilot for Outlook Tasks: Exercise Handout

**AI Developer & FDE Training | Kalpataru Projects, Ahmedabad**
Day 2, Block 5: Outlook | 25 minutes

In this exercise you will practise the Outlook skills from today's note: summarising a long thread, drafting a reply, adjusting tone and length, and writing a follow-up. If Copilot is not available inside your own Outlook, you will do the same tasks in Copilot Chat or Copilot in Word by pasting in the sample thread, which gives the same practice. You will work with one sample file about FreshCart, a made-up grocery delivery business. The business, people and numbers are all fictional.

By the end you will be able to:

1. Summarise a long, back-and-forth email thread
2. Draft a reply from a short instruction
3. Adjust a draft's tone and length without rewriting it
4. Write a follow-up that does not overstate what was agreed
5. Check a Copilot-drafted email before you send it

---

## Before You Start

- If Copilot is available in your Outlook, open the sample file, copy the messages into a test email thread in your own mailbox (a Draft folder or a message to yourself works), and use the real Outlook buttons.
- If Copilot is not available in your Outlook, open the sample file in Word and use Copilot in Word, or copy the text into Copilot Chat. The prompts below work either way; where a step names an Outlook button, an equivalent exists in Copilot Chat or Word.
- Use only the sample file. Do not paste real company emails into Copilot during this exercise.
- Read the whole thread once yourself before you start. It matters for this exercise, since some prompts ask you to check Copilot's answer against what you read.
- Copilot gives a different answer each time. Your result will not match your neighbour's.
- Work in pairs if you can: one person types the prompts, the other checks every answer against the thread. Swap halfway.

## How to Write a Good Prompt

Use the same four parts as in the earlier exercises.

| Part | Question it answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | What do I want? | "Summarise this thread" |
| Context | Who is it for, and why? | "For a colleague who has not seen it" |
| Source | What should Copilot use? | "Use only this thread" |
| Expectations | What shape, length and tone? | "Five bullet points, plain language" |

## Exercise A: Summarise the Thread

**Prompt A1**

> Summarise this email thread in six bullet points for someone who has not read it. Cover what is agreed, what is still open, and who owes whom a reply.

**Prompt A2: A sharper question**

> What is the current status of the 6 percent price increase? Is it agreed or not? Answer using only what is in the thread.

**Prompt A3: A second sharp question**

> Two different dates are mentioned for when weekly temperature logs will start. What are they, and who said each one?

**Think about it:** Did the summary in A1 state the price increase as settled? Compare that against your own answer to A2.

---

## Exercise B: Draft a Reply

The thread ends with Rohan's message on 30 September, which repeats a deadline of "30 September" for signing a 12-month contract, in the same message that arrives on 30 September.

**Prompt B1**

> Draft a reply to Rohan's most recent message. Do not agree to the 6 percent increase or the 12-month contract. Ask for more time to consider the 12-month option, and separately, ask again for one single confirmed date for the temperature logs. Keep it polite and professional, under 120 words.

**Prompt B2: A second reply**

> Draft a short reply to Priya's message where she says FreshCart's silence means the price increase is accepted. Correct this clearly but politely, and restate that the increase has not been agreed.

**Think about it:** Read both drafts against the thread. Did either one accidentally agree to something that is still open? Did either one miss the point it needed to make?

---

## Exercise C: Adjust Tone and Length

Take the draft from Prompt B2.

**Prompt C1**

> Make this firmer and more direct, while staying professional. Under 80 words.

**Prompt C2**

> Now make it warmer and more collaborative in tone, as if you want to keep a good working relationship while still being clear. Same length.

**Prompt C3**

> Rewrite this as a two-line message suitable for a chat app rather than an email.

**Think about it:** Which version would you actually send? Does the firmer version risk the relationship, and does the warmer version risk being too soft on the facts?

---

## Exercise D: Write a Follow-up

**Prompt D1**

> Write a short internal follow-up email to Arjun Shah, summarising where things stand after this thread: the price increase, the temperature log date, and the delivery trial. Say clearly what is agreed and what is still open. Do not repeat anything that is not actually stated in the thread.

**Prompt D2: A harder version**

> Do the same again, but this time list any statement in the thread that contradicts an earlier statement, and name who made each one.

**Think about it:** Did D1 surface the two contradictions on its own, or did you need the sharper instruction in D2 to get them?

---

## Exercise E: Check Before You Send

Use this checklist on every draft from Exercises B, C and D.

| Check | Done |
|---|---|
| Nothing is agreed that the thread does not actually agree | |
| Every date, percentage and name matches the thread | |
| Contradictions in the thread are flagged, not silently resolved | |
| The tone suits the reader and the relationship | |
| I have read the whole thread myself, not just the summary | |

If you cannot tick a box, correct the draft yourself or ask Copilot to fix it, then check again.

## Stretch Prompts

If you finish early, try these on the same thread.

> List every open question in this thread that nobody has answered yet.

> Write the reply you think Rohan is most likely to send next, based on the pattern of his previous replies, and label it clearly as a guess.

> If you had to escalate one issue in this thread to a manager, which one would it be, and why?

## Remember

- A long thread can be summarised in seconds, but a summary can just as quickly repeat a claim that was never agreed.
- Read for contradictions, not just content. Real threads often contain them.
- A draft that "sounds right" can still commit you to something you did not intend.
- You send the email, not Copilot. Read it first.
