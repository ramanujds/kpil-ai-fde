# Copilot in Word: Exercise Handout

**AI Developer & FDE Training | Kalpataru Projects, Ahmedabad**
Day 2, Block 2: Word | 30 minutes

In this exercise you will use Copilot in Word to draft, refine, summarise and improve text. You will work with three sample documents about FreshCart, a made-up grocery delivery business. The business, people and numbers are all fictional.

By the end you will be able to:

1. Draft a document from a short prompt
2. Change the tone, length and structure of existing text
3. Summarise a long document and pull out specific facts
4. Turn rough notes into a clear summary
5. Check Copilot's output before you share it

---

## Before You Start

- Sign in to Word with your work account and confirm you can see the Copilot button on the Home tab.
- Open the sample files from the folder your trainer shares with you.
- Use only these sample files. Do not paste real company documents, names or figures into Copilot during this exercise.
- Your screen may look slightly different from a colleague's. Copilot's buttons change between versions, so look for the Copilot icon rather than an exact menu path.
- Copilot gives a different answer each time. Your result will not match your neighbour's, and that is normal. It is a good reason to compare and check.
- Work in pairs if you can: one person types the prompts, the other reads every result critically. Swap halfway.

## How to Write a Good Prompt

Every prompt in this handout has four parts. Use the same four parts when you write your own.

| Part | Question it answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | What do I want? | "Write a one-page update" |
| Context | Who is it for, and why? | "For the procurement lead, ahead of a contract decision" |
| Source | What should Copilot use? | "Use only this document" |
| Expectations | What shape, length and tone? | "Formal, under 200 words, bullet points" |

If a result is not what you wanted, do not start over. Reply to Copilot and say what to change, for example "shorter", "more formal" or "keep the numbers".

## Exercise A: Draft from a Prompt

Open a new blank Word document. You do not need a sample file.

**Prompt A1**

> Write a one-page internal memo announcing that FreshCart will trial an earlier 5 am delivery window from a dairy supplier for two weeks. The audience is warehouse staff. Explain what changes, why we are trying it, and what we need from them. Friendly but clear. Under 250 words. End with a question they can reply to.

**Then try**

- Ask Copilot to add a short section called "What happens if the trial does not work".
- Ask for three alternative subject lines for the memo.

**Think about it:** Copilot did not know the real reasons, dates or names. Did it add any details that you never gave it?

## Exercise B: Refine Tone, Length and Structure

Open FreshCart_Q3_Supplier_Performance_Update.docx. Read it quickly first so you know what it says.

**Prompt B1: Change the audience**

> Rewrite section 4.4 (Harvest Ridge Produce) as a short, friendly note to the supplier's owner. Keep the facts, but make the tone constructive rather than critical. Under 120 words.

**Prompt B2: Change the length**

> Shorten section 2 (Background) to two sentences, keeping every number.

**Prompt B3: Change the structure**

> Turn section 6 (Recommendations) into a table with three columns: Recommendation, Supplier, and Why.

**Prompt B4: Change the format**

> Rewrite section 5 (Risks) as three bullet points, most serious first, each starting with a bold risk name.

**Think about it:** Compare each result with the original section. Is every fact and number still there, and does it still mean the same thing?

## Exercise C: Summarise a Long Document

Stay in FreshCart_Q3_Supplier_Performance_Update.docx.

**Prompt C1: Executive summary**

> Summarise this document in five bullet points for a busy manager who will only read the summary. Put the decision needed first.

**Prompt C2: Facts in a table**

> List every supplier with its on-time delivery percentage and number of complaints, in a table. Use only figures stated in the document.

**Prompt C3: A specific question**

> Which supplier had the most problems and what caused them? Answer in three sentences.

**Prompt C4: A different reader**

> Write a three-sentence summary for a customer support agent who needs to explain late fruit deliveries to customers.

**Think about it:** Check every number in your summaries against the document. Is there anything in a summary that you cannot find in the file?

## Exercise D: Turn Rough Notes into a Formal Summary

Open FreshCart_Coastal_Dairy_Meeting_Notes_Messy.docx. These are one person's quick notes from a supplier meeting. They are incomplete and untidy, as real notes usually are.

**Prompt D1: First attempt**

> Turn these rough meeting notes into a formal meeting summary with these headings: Attendees, Key Discussion Points, Decisions Made, and Action Items. Include an owner and a due date for each action item.

Read the result carefully before moving on. Compare the owners and dates with what the notes actually say.

**Prompt D2: Second attempt with a guardrail**

> Redo the meeting summary using only what is in the notes. Add an "Open Questions" section. Where something is unclear, missing or disputed, write "To be confirmed" instead of guessing. In the action items table, use the columns Action, Owner, Due Date, and write "Not stated" where the notes do not give an owner or date.

**Prompt D3: Follow-up email**

> Write a short thank-you and follow-up email to the supplier based on this summary. Do not commit FreshCart to anything that was not agreed in the meeting.

**Think about it:** What changed between the first and second attempt? What did the second prompt ask for that the first one did not?

## Exercise E: Review and Improve Existing Text

Open FreshCart_Delivery_Delay_Notice_Draft.docx. This is a first draft of a message to customers. Read it once yourself before you use Copilot.

**Prompt E1: Find the problems first**

> Review this customer notice. List the problems you find under these headings: Clarity, Tone, Grammar and Spelling, Consistency, and Missing Information. Do not rewrite anything yet.

**Prompt E2: Rewrite**

> Rewrite this notice for customers. Lead with the apology and the key message, keep it under 180 words, use plain language, and keep the facts unchanged.

**Prompt E3: Tighten**

> Make the rewritten version 30 percent shorter without losing the discount offer.

**Prompt E4: Second opinion**

> Read the rewritten notice as an angry customer whose order was late. What would still annoy you? List three points.

**Think about it:** Did Copilot's review find everything you found on your own read-through? Did it find anything you missed? Read the final version once more yourself before you call it done.

## Exercise F: Check Before You Share

Use this checklist on the outputs from Exercises B to E. It is the same habit you will use for real work documents.

| Check | Done |
|---|---|
| Every number matches the source document | |
| No names, dates or owners were invented | |
| Nothing is promised that the source does not support | |
| The tone suits the reader | |
| The length and format match what I asked for | |
| I have read the whole output myself | |

If you cannot tick a box, correct the text yourself or ask Copilot to fix it, then check again.

## Stretch Prompts

If you finish early, try these on any of the sample documents.

- "Ask me five questions that a manager might raise after reading this document, then draft answers using only the document."
- "Suggest a better title and a one-line subtitle for this report."
- "Add a glossary of any terms a new joiner might not know."
- "Which parts of this document are weakest, and how would you strengthen them?"

## Remember

- Copilot writes quickly and sounds confident, even when it is wrong.
- The better your prompt, the less you need to fix afterwards.
- You are responsible for anything you share, so review every output first.
